# Combat candidate 1 — money and farming measurements

Generated by `scripts/combat-economics.py` on the reference contract with fake QU; conservation asserted after every run. Bots: reader-v1 (stronger) and mixed-v1 (weaker), 1,000 QU stake. Execution fees come from the simulated fee model (`chainsim.FeeModel`: 10 QU per call, 1 per tick, 20 per resolved round), a candidate, not a measured Qubic cost. Hosting and provider costs are not included.

Exec/fight counts the two entries, the commits and reveals sent and the resolved rounds; the ticks a fight shares with others add about 20 QU in the arena. At a 1,000 QU stake the house's rake share never covers it.

Net per fight is per group of three fighters, divided by all contests. With no subsidy the players as a whole lose exactly the rake; skill moves QU from weaker to stronger bots. The game must not be promoted as guaranteed earnings.

Every row stops at 30 contests: six fighters form 15 pairs, and the cap of two rated starts per pair per epoch allows exactly 30. With a small launch population, the pair cap rather than demand limits ranked volume: N fighters can play at most N(N-1) ranked fights per epoch.

## Break-even stake

Execution cost per ranked fight: **188 QU** (measured in the demo arena below). At 500 bps rake and a 60% house share the house breaks even from a stake of **3,137 QU**; a player breaks even at a win share of **0.526** (draws refund).

The known-owner exclusion held (no same-owner pairing). Distinct-owner accomplices are not detectable by the contract: the pair cap (two rated starts per pair per epoch) and the 120-tick rematch gap bound the gain per accomplice, so a larger ring buys faster farming at a QU cost of the rake per thrown fight. These are mitigations, not sybil resistance (competition.md §3).

**Findings (2026-09-26):**
- **House.** Execution costs about 190 QU per fight in every mode and does not
  depend on the stake. At a 1,000 QU tier the house loses about 160 QU per fight;
  at the 5,000 QU tier with tier-scaled duel stakes and cup entries it makes
  about +90 per fight, and every mode is positive (ranked +85, cup +39 after
  sponsorship, duel +22 over 20k ticks). That meets the AUD-018 target under the
  candidate fee model.
- **Players.** Players as a whole still lose the rake. A bot scoring above 0.55
  makes about +20% of its stake per ranked fight (+23.6% over 20k ticks); one at
  0.5 loses 2.5% (half the rake). The break-even win share is 0.526.
  `economics.json` publishes this per tier.
- **Farming.** Duel series fell from 62 to 18 in 20k ticks: weak bots stop
  accepting and issuing challenges against a fighter that keeps beating them.
  tengu's duel winnings fell from 56 tier-stakes to 15 over the same run. Cups
  now draw the strong ranked bots. The sponsorship cap withheld sponsorship
  from 2 of the 6 cups while kappa, an adaptive search bot, won 4. A capped
  house transfer, not a farmed one.
- **Ratings and seasons.** After AUD-014 the spread is stable, not widening:
  a range of about 600, mean drift 6–9 points per 1,000 ticks in the last third. In
  20k ticks the most common pair was 3.2% of ranked fights, against 5.4% before,
  and every season champion was the top-rated fighter with the top 3
  qualified. Before, S1 went to the third-rated fighter and the second-rated
  failed qualification. The top-rated pair met in 15% of their fights; in a
  field of 15 that is expected, not isolation. The 100..200 rating window is
  contract spec and was left unchanged.
- **Market.** Priced: 5 sales in 20k ticks, median 79,034 QU, range 62,046 to
  292,337, from a public value model. It shows asks, prices and fees; it does
  not measure demand.
