# The Qubic riddle pack

Three riddle families that ask a fighter to read Qubic the way a node does:
decode a transaction frame, replay an asset ledger, audit a nested contract
call. They are synthetic, generated by the house like every other riddle, and
opt-in: a classic round is untouched unless `house spar` is started with
`--riddle-pack`. This guide records where the rules come from, what each
family simplifies, the candid review of what each one is worth (#16), what
was measured on fresh instances (#17), and how to put the pack in front of
fighters. The code is
[`qubic_riddles.py`](../packages/qdojo/src/qdojo/qubic_riddles.py); the
selection lives in [`riddles.py`](../packages/qdojo/src/qdojo/riddles.py).

## Provenance

Every rule is pinned to Qubic core revision
`9896264e9de2224bd30be71248eeba9077b56203` and to three files at that
revision:

- [`src/network_messages/transactions.h`](https://github.com/qubic/core/blob/9896264e9de2224bd30be71248eeba9077b56203/src/network_messages/transactions.h):
  the transaction layout. An 80-byte header (32-byte source public key,
  32-byte destination public key, `int64` amount, `uint32` tick, `uint16`
  inputType, `uint16` inputSize), then inputSize payload bytes, then a 64-byte
  signature. `Transaction::checkValidity()` requires `0 <= amount <=
  MAX_AMOUNT` and `inputSize <= 1024`.
- [`doc/contracts.md`](https://github.com/qubic/core/blob/9896264e9de2224bd30be71248eeba9077b56203/doc/contracts.md):
  originator versus invocator, the per-call invocation reward, cross-contract
  calls only to a lower contract index, and ownership and possession versus
  management of shares.
- [`src/qpi/qpi_context.h`](https://github.com/qubic/core/blob/9896264e9de2224bd30be71248eeba9077b56203/src/qpi/qpi_context.h):
  `transferShareOwnershipAndPossession` takes one `newOwnerAndPossessor`, so
  a transfer moves both roles to the same recipient.

`MAX_AMOUNT` is `ISSUANCE_RATE * 1000`, 10^15, from
`src/network_messages/common_def.h` at the same revision. The QPI headers
moved before this revision: `src/contracts/qpi.h` and
`src/contract_core/qpi.h` return 404, the declarations are under `src/qpi/`.

Every statement carries its complete rules, and the version is in the
title and in the input; all of it sits inside the hashed public fields, so
an instance needs no lookup outside the riddle. A change to a family's rules
is a new version; version 1 has never been fought live, which is why the
rule changes from the review stayed at version 1.

## The families

| belt | kind | asks for | the Qubic fact that decides it |
|---|---|---|---|
| orange | `qubic_transaction_audit` | a sum or count over transaction frames that pass validity and a query | the wire layout: signed amount, unsigned tick, inputSize fixes the length, `0 <= amount <= MAX_AMOUNT` |
| green | `qubic_asset_ledger` | one identity's shares of one asset after a journal is replayed | a transfer moves ownership and possession together to one recipient; a management event moves only the manager |
| blue | `qubic_call_audit` | accepted audits, or refunds or credits to one identity, over nested call traces | originator is constant through nesting, invocator is the immediate caller, the reward belongs to the current frame, a nested call must go to a lower index |

All three answer with a single decimal integer; the statement says exact
arithmetic, a sum may exceed 64 bits and must not wrap.

### qubic_transaction_audit (orange)

Each hex frame is an independent transaction in the wire layout above. Keep
a frame only if it has at least 80 bytes, its total length is exactly
`80 + inputSize + 64`, and its amount is between 0 and `MAX_AMOUNT`
inclusive. Filter the kept frames by the query's destination, input type,
inclusive tick range and, when the query has one, source. Then sum the
amount, count the frames, or sum the first eight payload bytes of each as a
`uint64` (a payload shorter than eight bytes contributes nothing).

Simplifications, all stated in the riddle: signatures are dummy bytes, their
presence is structural and their content irrelevant; keys are raw hex, not
base-26 Qubic identities, and no digest or signature is checked; input types
are arbitrary values that select nothing; the `inputSize <= 1024` clause of
`checkValidity()` is not enforced, because exercising it needs frames over
1 KB. Every instance carries every trap once (a negative amount, one above
`MAX_AMOUNT`, one exactly at it, a frame one byte short and one byte long, a
header short of 80 bytes, a payload shorter than eight bytes, frames just
outside both ends of the tick range) and then a random mixture.

### qubic_asset_ledger (green)

A holding lives in a slot `(issuer, name, owner, possessor, manager)`. Sum
the fragmented initial records per slot, then replay the journal in order.
Each event names its exact `from` slot and its shares; the destination is
derived: a transfer's destination keeps issuer, name and manager and has the
recipient as both owner and possessor; a management event's destination
keeps everything but the manager. If `settled` is true every event carries a
status and rejected ones do nothing; if it is false there are no statuses and
an event applies iff its from slot holds at least its shares at that moment.
The query sums one asset's shares where the named role equals the identity,
optionally under one manager; empty holdings count as zero.

Simplifications, all stated: this is a bounded normalized journal, not raw
Qubic logs and not a simulation of authorization, callbacks or transfer
fees. One manager denotes both the ownership-managing and the
possession-managing contract, which the real `acquireShares` and
`releaseShares` keep separate. No issuance or burn occurs in a journal; the
supply is constant and every replay is checked to keep it so. Asset identity
is the pair (issuer, name): two issuers share the ticker `DOJO`.

### qubic_call_audit (blue)

Each transaction is a trace of `enter`, `audit` and `exit` events under one
originating user. A stack of frames gives an audit its three facts: the
originator is the transaction's user throughout, the invocator is the parent
frame's contract (or the user at the top level), and `invocationReward` is
the reward on the current frame, restored when a child returns. The intended
auditor accepts iff the originator equals `allowed` and the current reward is
at least `fee`; an accepted audit refunds reward minus fee to the invocator
and credits the reward to the originator. A trace in which a nested `enter`
names a contract index not lower than the frame it is entered from is
invalid and none of its audits count. The query counts accepted audits or
sums refunds or credits to one identity.

Simplifications, all stated: receipts are independent calculations, not
transfers or balance mutations; the refund is hypothetical, real Qubic does
not return unused `invocationReward`; no callbacks, system procedures or
execution fees are modelled, and every call is assumed completed and funded.
`buggy_auditor` is pseudocode that checks the invocator and uses the root
reward; the statement gives the intended rule in full, so the catalogue's
allowance to solve the specification instead of the code applies and the
reference solver never reads it. Contract indices stay in 10..29, under the
pinned `contractCount` of 30.

## The input envelope

The public riddle is the usual five fields (`round_id`, `title`,
`statement`, `input`, `answer_format`), so `riddle_hash` covers everything
below. For a pack riddle `input` is a JSON string holding one object:

```json
{"family": "qubic_asset_ledger", "version": 1,
 "settled": true,
 "initial": [{"slot": {"issuer": "I1", "name": "DOJO", "owner": "U2", "possessor": "U3", "manager": 1}, "shares": 1434}],
 "journal": [{"operation": "management", "from": {"issuer": "I1", "name": "DOJO", "owner": "U4", "possessor": "U1", "manager": 1},
              "new_manager": 2, "shares": 296, "status": "rejected"},
             {"operation": "transfer", "from": {"issuer": "I1", "name": "DOJO", "owner": "U1", "possessor": "U2", "manager": 1},
              "recipient": "U2", "shares": 24, "status": "applied"}],
 "query": {"issuer": "I1", "name": "CUP", "role": "possessor", "identity": "U1", "manager": null}}
```

That is the first record, the first two events and the query of the seed-7
green sample, laid out for reading; the real string is compact, with keys
sorted.

`family` names the generator and is what a solver should dispatch on, never
the title; `version` is the rule revision, 1 for all three. The rest is the
family's data and its `query`:

| family | data | query |
|---|---|---|
| `qubic_transaction_audit` | `frames`: hex strings | `destination` (hex), `input_type`, `tick_min`, `tick_max`, `metric` (`amount`, `count` or `payload_u64`), optional `source` (hex) |
| `qubic_asset_ledger` | `settled` (bool), `initial`: `[{slot, shares}]`, `journal`: events with `operation` (`transfer` or `management`), `from` slot, `shares`, `recipient` or `new_manager`, and `status` only when settled | `issuer`, `name`, `role` (`owner` or `possessor`), `identity`, `manager` (int or null) |
| `qubic_call_audit` | `transactions`: `[{originator, events}]` with events `{op: enter, contract, reward}`, `{op: audit, allowed, fee}`, `{op: exit}`; `buggy_auditor`: a string | `metric` (`accepted`, `refund` or `credited`), `identity` |

The titles are `Orange belt: Qubic transaction audit v1`, `Green belt:
Qubic asset ledger v1` and `Blue belt: Qubic call audit v1`, stable per
family and version. The answer is an integer and can be zero.

## Commands

```bash
uv run qdojo riddle list --pack qubic
uv run qdojo riddle list --pack mixed
uv run qdojo riddle sample qubic_call_audit --rng-seed 7
uv run qdojo riddle sample qubic_asset_ledger --rng-seed 7 --with-answer
uv run qdojo riddle sample qubic_transaction_audit --rng-seed 7 --round-id 42
```

`riddle list` prints the kinds per belt for a pack: `qubic` has nothing at
white or yellow, `mixed` shows the three beside the classic kinds. `riddle
sample` prints the public fields, exactly the JSON a solver receives on
stdin; `--with-answer` prints the authored document with the answer, for a
local fixture; `--round-id` defaults to 1 and must be positive. `--rng-seed`
is a practice generator seed, not a wallet seed. Pipe a sample into a solver
to try it: `uv run qdojo riddle sample qubic_call_audit --rng-seed 7 |
python3 examples/solvers/qubic_pack.py` prints `{"answer": 54777}`.

The reference solvers are
[`examples/solvers/qubic_transaction_audit.py`](../examples/solvers/qubic_transaction_audit.py),
[`qubic_asset_ledger.py`](../examples/solvers/qubic_asset_ledger.py) and
[`qubic_call_audit.py`](../examples/solvers/qubic_call_audit.py), each
written from its statement alone and each 27..30 lines of logic.
[`qubic_pack.py`](../examples/solvers/qubic_pack.py) reads `family` from
the input and runs the matching one; on any other riddle, classic ones
included, it prints nothing and the bot sits the round out. One command
therefore covers the three tables:

```bash
uv run qdojo bot run --board URL --solver python3 examples/solvers/qubic_pack.py --interval 7
```

`scripts/riddle-pack-measure.py` is the measurement harness (`solve`,
`reuse`, `spar`, `all`); it runs offline, signs nothing, and its frozen
first-look solvers are under `scripts/riddle-pack/first-look/`.

## The review, per family

From issue #16. Method: instances at seeds 7 and 3 were read with and
without answers; the Qubic facts were checked against the pinned core; an
independent solver was written from the public JSON and one rule at a time
was broken over 200 fresh seeds to see which rules decide the answer.
"Before" is commit `cd991ef`, "after" is `7aafcec`, the commit that acted on
the review.

### qubic_transaction_audit

**What decides the answer.** The real 80-byte header, little-endian, then
inputSize payload bytes and a 64-byte signature: the exact struct in
`transactions.h`. Three things a careless parser gets wrong all decide the
answer: the amount is signed (a `uint64` reader keeps negative frames), the
tick is unsigned and above 2^31 in every instance (a signed-32 reader
rejects or mis-orders it), and inputSize fixes the total length, so a frame
one byte short or long is rejected. After the change the validity rule is
the pinned `Transaction::checkValidity()` minus its `inputSize <= 1024`
clause, with a frame at exactly `MAX_AMOUNT` retained and one above it
rejected in every instance.

**What is decoration.** The signature bytes, the payload beyond its first
eight bytes, the inputType values (1, 257, 4096, 65535 are arbitrary; real
types select procedures and nothing here depends on that), and identities
(raw hex, stated as such; no base-26 encoding, no K12 digest, no signature
check). The source key was pure decoration before; it is now queried in
half the instances.

**Does it teach Qubic?** It teaches what a transaction looks like on the
wire and that inputSize drives the frame length. A parser written for it
decodes real broadcast transactions. It does not teach identities, digests
or signatures, and says so.

**Reusable solver versus repetition.** Firmly on the reusable-solver side,
with almost no rule variation: every instance deliberately carries every
trap, so a parser that passes one seed passes all. That is the catalogue's
"fresh instance" routine play. Added variation: `MAX_AMOUNT`, the optional
source filter, and tick-range widths of 0, 1, 20 or 2..60 (a width-0 range
in about a quarter of instances). Candidates not added: a destination given
as a contract index (needs the contract-key-equals-index rule, which could
not be verified from the pinned `qubic.cpp`), `inputSize <= 1024` (needs
frames over 1 KB), a K12 digest metric (too heavy for orange).

**Canonical?** Yes: sums and counts, no ordering, no ties. Inclusive tick
bounds are hit on both ends every instance; framing off-by-one is tested on
both sides; short payloads are retained but contribute 0 to `payload_u64`.
Counterexample found and fixed: the statement said "All arithmetic is exact;
no overflow" while 51 of 109 `payload_u64` answers over seeds 0..299
exceeded 2^64 (99 exceeded 2^63). A C, Rust or Go solver summing in
`uint64` wrapped silently, and "no overflow" read as a promise that it would
not. The suffix now says a sum may exceed 64 bits and must not wrap.

**Size.** Input field 5315..8306 bytes before, 5658..8060 after (median 6942
to 7012). A frame is about 300 hex characters of which 128 are signature
and 64 are source key; the length rule needs those bytes to exist, so the
size is the real wire format times 18..25 frames. Not padding, but only
about a dozen frames do distinct work; the rest vary order and mixture, and
were trimmed from 5..14 to 3..10 to pay for the three new fixed frames.

### qubic_asset_ledger

**Before: generic bookkeeping in Qubic names.** Every journal row spelled
out both the from slot and the to slot in full, so the reference solver was
six lines: sum the initial rows matching the query, then add
`shares * (matches(to) - matches(from))` for each applied row. The
statement's rule that a transfer sets owner and possessor to the same
recipient was true and unused; nothing about ownership versus possession
versus management decided anything. Queries were always drawn from a live
holding, so the stated "unknown holdings count as zero" never happened.

**After: the Qubic fact decides.** Rows carry `from`, `shares` and either
`recipient` or `new_manager`; the destination slot is derived.
`qpi_context.h`'s `transferShareOwnershipAndPossession` takes one
`newOwnerAndPossessor`, so a solver that moves ownership only gets 71 of 96
possessor queries wrong (and 15 of 104 owner queries, through later
from-slots that no longer exist). A management event moves the manager and
nothing else. The query is the shape of `qpi.numberOfShares(asset,
AssetOwnershipSelect, AssetPossessionSelect)`: by owner or by possessor,
optionally by managing contract. The stated simplification stands: one
manager denotes both managing contracts, although the real `acquireShares`
and `releaseShares` carry separate ownership- and possession-managing
indices.

**Reusable solver versus repetition.** Two rule variants now, chosen per
instance by the `settled` flag. Settled: statuses are given and must be
read; rejections are half overdraws and half not, so overdraw is not the
tell, and the query leans on a holding that a rejected event would have
moved (ignoring statuses changes the answer in 55 of 106 settled instances,
up from 17). Unsettled: no statuses; an event applies iff its exact source
slot holds at least its shares at that point, the real reason a transfer
fails, which forces a state replay in journal order. Every unsettled
instance carries an overdraw rejection and an application of exactly what
was held; 93 of 94 also a rejection from a drained slot. Further candidates:
separate ownership and possession managers (the real model), issuance
events adding supply, queries by managing contract alone.

**Canonical?** Sums only. Boundaries: `shares == held` applies ("at
least"), `held + 1` rejects, a drained slot holds 0, a self-transfer nets
zero, manager null versus a value. Zero answers now occur and are stated.
No ambiguity was found beyond the never-exercised zero case.

**Size.** This was the largest family, not blue: 7481..10907 bytes before,
5606..8524 after (median 8991 to 6948). The removed bytes were the
spelled-out to slots: padding in the strict sense, since they carried what
the rule should have produced.

### qubic_call_audit

**What decides the answer.** Three verified facts from `contracts.md`:
originator is the user who signed the transaction and is constant through
nesting; invocator is the immediate caller, the user at top level and the
calling contract below; invocationReward is the reward on the current
frame, so context must be restored when a child returns. A fourth is now
enforced: a PUBLIC procedure "can be called by other contracts with larger
contract index", so a trace whose nested enter names an index not lower
than its caller's is invalid and counts for nothing (the generator always
obeyed this; now 87 of 200 instances break it once). Load-bearing as
measured: using the root reward changes the answer in 167 of 200 instances,
checking invocator instead of originator in 171 of 200, ignoring validity in
61 of 87 instances that carry an invalid trace. Refund queries name a
contract label in 41 of 200, which is where "invocator is a contract" bites.

**What is decoration.** `buggy_auditor`: the intended rule is stated in
full, so nobody needs to read the pseudocode. The refund is hypothetical and
stated so; real Qubic does not return unused invocationReward. The identity
in an `accepted` query is ignored and stated so. Contract indices are 10..29
to stay under the pinned contractCount of 30, which nothing depends on.

**Reusable solver versus repetition.** Before, every frame had the same
four audits in the same order with the same outcomes, 150 events per
instance to make four points 28 times; a stack solver that passed one frame
passed all. Now each frame draws 1..3 probes from {accepted with partial
refund, rejected above the reward, accepted at exactly the reward, rejected
because allowed is the parent contract} and a 70 % post-children probe,
with the nested invocator trap and a depth-1 restoration probe with a refund
at stake forced once per instance inside a trace that counts. Median events
per instance 150 to 118. Rule variants now: the validity rule. Not modelled
and stated so: callbacks, system procedures, execution fees.

**Canonical?** Sums and counts, no ties. `fee == reward` accepts with refund
0; `fee == reward + 1` rejects; both are in every frame's probe pool. The
same contract can be entered twice in one transaction (siblings or
different depths); labels are identities and refunds sum, which is
canonical. "An empty sum is zero" was an unstated convention and is now
stated. An earlier draft of the query bias produced 24 % zeros, which would
have let a fighter answering 0 win a quarter of blue rounds; the final draft
leans on the `accepted` metric instead when an invalid trace exists.

**Size.** The issue's "~8 KB at seed 7" is the CLI's public JSON, statement
included: 7710 bytes before, 7711 after, of which the statement is 1599.
The input field at seed 7 is 5120 to 4880; across seeds 3574..7978 to
2658..6545 (median 5770 to 4504). Each event is about 40 characters of JSON
for three facts; the object form is worth keeping for readability, and the
repetition of identical probes was the padding.

### Fake-chain spar, one round per family

`payout_mode first`, entry fee 1000, commit window 50 ticks, reveal 20,
FakeChain schedule offset 5 ticks; bots poll through `Bot.step`, solvers
through `solver.run_solver`. `delay` is how many ticks after the riddle
became visible a bot first polled; every bot polled every tick after that.

## Belt calibration

By every deterministic measure the three families are indistinguishable:
200/200 solved, the same 25 ms median, first-look 50/50, solver logic of
27..30 lines each, and all cheaper to compute than classic green's
`pow_nonce`. The measured differences are the zero share (a zero-bot wins
about 8..10 % of green and blue rounds and none of orange) and input size
(blue smallest, green largest on stdin). The provisional orange < green <
blue order therefore holds only as a reading-and-modelling ladder: orange is
stateless (one frame at a time, seven independent clauses), green needs
sequential state with a derived destination and two status modes, blue
needs nested state with context restoration and a trace-level validity that
discards work. Nothing measured contradicts that order, nothing confirms it
either, and it should be re-checked against the first live rounds and one
LLM run per family, which the measurement stage could not make. Until then
the belts are provisional.

## Handing the pack to a simulation

The house opts in per spar; nothing else changes. `--riddle-pack qubic`
with `--belts orange,green,blue` isolates the three families:

```bash
uv run qdojo --node IP house --data private/house spar --riddle-pack qubic --belts orange,green,blue \
    --rounds 30 --entry-fee 1000 --min-players 3 --payout-mode first --poll 15
```

`--riddle-pack mixed` keeps the classic belts and adds each family at its
belt; the kind is drawn uniformly from the belt's pool, so a Qubic riddle
comes up in one orange round in five, one green round in four and one blue
round in two. `--riddle-pack qubic` with white or yellow in `--belts` is an
error at start-up, before the riddle directory is created or a round opened,
not a fallback to classic. The spar still skips a belt no outsider can sit at unless
`--dead-tables` is given, and fighters sit at their belt or above, so a
white-belt bot can enter every pack table.

Before a watched run:

- The pack has to be in the checkout the house runs from. It lives on a
  branch until merged; the house process does not pick up a worktree.
- The deployed page only changes when `apps/web/data` is committed and
  pushed to `main` (see [docs/operations.md](operations.md)). Export, commit
  and push before the first round opens, or the public site shows nothing
  while the round runs.
- `apps/web/llms.txt` does not mention the pack, and its hash is signed on
  chain, so telling coding agents about it is a re-sign, not an edit.
- Never pass `--rng-seed` to a live spar. That seed drives both the kind
  draw and every instance, and the generator is public: a known seed
  reveals every answer in advance. Unset, the spar seeds from the operating
  system. The practice seeds 7 and 3 are public and must not be fought for
  money.
- The riddle files the spar writes under `<data>/riddles/` carry the answer
  and are mode 0600; they stay private until settlement.

Reading the result:

- Every row of `<data>/metrics.jsonl` carries `riddle_pack` beside `kind`
  and `title`, so pack rounds can be filtered out of a mixed run.
  `qdojo house metrics` summarises by belt, not by kind: read pack-only
  numbers off the rows.
- `payout_mode: first` pays everyone tied in the earliest commit tick, and
  identical deterministic bots on one poll interval tie every round, so the
  harness sets the result, not the content. The spar table above reproduces
  the round-119 tie on every family: two twins split 1925/1925 while a bot
  polling three ticks later is `solved` and unpaid. Stagger `--interval`
  across bots or mix solvers before reading any ranking off the data.
- A bot that prints 0 solves every zero-answer round and commits before any
  real solver. Zeros are about 8 % of green and 9 % of blue instances, none
  of orange; a solve rate read off a spar with such a bot in it measures the
  zero share, not the content.
- There is no `--chain fake` in the CLI: `house spar` signs and needs
  `--node`. To fight a pack round without the live chain, drive
  `Spar.one_round` on `FakeChain` from Python the way
  `test_qubic_spar_round_commits_reveals_settles_and_exports` does, or run
  `scripts/riddle-pack-measure.py spar`.
- Economic Monte Carlo (`qdojo house model`, [docs/model.md](model.md)) is
  not solver testing. Keep the two apart.

## What is not proven

- **Public seeds reveal answers.** The generator is public and
  deterministic, so any instance whose seed is known is solved by running
  it. Practice samples are for practice; live instances must come from a
  seed nobody can know, which is what an unset `--rng-seed` gives. Private
  generation remains a trust boundary.
- **Hashes bind, they do not prove fairness.** `riddle_hash` binds the
  publisher to the document and `answer_commitment` to one answer; together
  they prove the goalposts did not move. They do not prove that the answer
  is correct, that the instance was generated as the statement says, or
  that it was secret before publish. The evidence for correctness is
  house-side: the tests reconstruct every answer from the public JSON by a
  different route, and the reference solvers, written from the statements,
  solve 200 of 200 fresh instances per family.
- **Nothing has been fought live.** Every round so far ran on `FakeChain`.
  The first live rounds over the native chain, and one LLM run per family,
  are what the belt order and the solve rates should be re-checked against.
- **What the families do not model**, all stated in their riddles:
  signatures, digests and encoded identities; the `inputSize <= 1024`
  clause; a destination given as a contract index; separate ownership- and
  possession-managing contracts; issuance and burns; callbacks, system
  procedures and execution fees; refunds of unused invocation reward, which
  real Qubic does not make. Passing these riddles says a solver reads the
  wire layout, the share-transfer rule and the call context correctly. It
  does not say the solver can sign, verify or run a contract.
